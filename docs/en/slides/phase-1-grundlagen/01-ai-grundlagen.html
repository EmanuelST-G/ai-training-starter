AI Fundamentals

Phase 1 — What is AI, and when is it the right choice?

Learning Objective

  • Be able to explain what AI is and what it is not.
  • Distinguish classical software, machine learning and generative AI.
  • Name the most important AI application types.
  • Understand why AI models make mistakes.

What Is AI?

Artificial intelligence refers to software that takes on tasks for which humans typically require intelligence: understanding language, recognising patterns, making decisions.

In practice today, "AI" almost always means machine learning: models that generalise from data instead of being explicitly programmed.

Distinction: Classical Software → GenAI

ApproachProperties
Classical softwareDeterministic, rule-based
Rule-based systems"If X, then Y" logic, often with a knowledge base
Machine learningLearns patterns from examples
Deep learningML with deep neural networks
Generative AICreates new content (text, image, code)

Training, Inference, Model

  • Training: model learns from data (one-off or continuous)
  • Inference: model produces predictions for new inputs
  • Model: stored result of training — the "compiled experience"

So data flows through the model twice: when learning and when answering.

Structured vs. Unstructured Data

TypeExamples
StructuredTables, databases, clearly defined schema
UnstructuredTexts, images, audio, video

AI can process both — but models differ greatly in what they are suitable for.

Probabilistic Outputs

Unlike classical software, AI returns a probability distribution over possible answers.

Two practical consequences:

  1. The same request can yield slightly different answers.
  2. Statements often sound confident but are not guaranteed to be correct.

Why AI Makes Mistakes

  • Bias in the training data — the model inherits prejudices
  • Distribution shift — the world changes, the model does not
  • Hallucinations — the model "invents" plausible content
  • Lack of currency — the model knows nothing after its training cutoff

Overview: AI Application Types

  • Classification — spam or not?
  • Forecasting — sales figures next week?
  • Clustering — which customers are similar?
  • Recommender systems — which film fits?
  • Computer vision — what is in the image?
  • NLP — understand, generate, translate text
  • Generative AI — create new content

Take-aways

  • "AI" is an umbrella term — the question is always: which AI?
  • Probabilistic outputs are the normal case, not the error.
  • Data quality beats model quality in most projects.
  • Not every problem is an AI problem — classical software often wins.
